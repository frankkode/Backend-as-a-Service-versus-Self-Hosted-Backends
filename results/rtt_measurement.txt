Network path measurement — client to each benchmark endpoint
Measured 2026-09-03 from the benchmark client machine (same machine used for all k6 runs).
Method: curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{time_connect}" — TCP handshake time, approximately one RTT,
excluding TLS negotiation and application processing. Five consecutive samples per endpoint.

VPS, Hostinger KVM 2, Germany/Frankfurt (port 8080, HTTP):
  58.9  51.2  96.7  65.2  53.2 ms
  median 58.9   min 51.2   mean 65.0

Supabase project, AWS eu-west-2 / London (HTTPS):
  33.7  44.2  22.5  24.4  25.8 ms
  median 25.8   min 22.5   mean 30.1

Median difference: 33.0 ms, with the self-hosted (Django) endpoint the farther of the two.

Interpretation for Section 3.6 / 6.3:
The two variants are not co-located. The residual round-trip difference of ~33 ms disadvantages
the self-hosted variant, i.e. it works against rather than in favour of the low-concurrency
finding reported in Section 5.1. At the 50 and 200 VU levels, where measured latencies are in the
seconds range, a 33 ms path difference is immaterial; it is only comparable to the effect size at
the 10 VU level.

This measurement supersedes the earlier configuration in which the Django variant was reached over
loopback (DJANGO_BASE_URL=http://localhost/api) while Supabase was reached over the public
internet — a condition under which the network asymmetry favoured the self-hosted variant by
approximately the full Supabase RTT.
